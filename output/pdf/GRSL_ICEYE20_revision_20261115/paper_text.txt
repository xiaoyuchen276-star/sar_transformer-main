SAR Despeckling via Fourier Reﬁnement and
Representation-Consistent Reconstruction
Hongyu Chen, Peng Liu, Senior Member , IEEE
Abstract—We extend Trans-SAR with full-spectrum Fourier
reﬁnement and representation-consistent reconstruction to ad-
dress the gap between simulated speckle and real synthetic
aperture radar (SAR) observations. One bottleneck and ﬁve de-
coder blocks combine local features with real–imaginary channel
mixing. Bounded log-intensity predictions are inverted before in-
tensity compensation. Masked adaptation updates reconstruction
on noisy real SAR while freezing the Transformer encoder . On
8,400 simulated pairs from 21 UCMerced classes and four look
numbers, the blind model achieves macro-averaged peak signal-
to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index (SSIM)
values of 28.14 dB and 0.7791, leading PSNR at every look.
Removing Fourier reﬁnement reduces PSNR by 1.68 dB. On 20
external ICEYE scenes, adaptation lowers mean M-index from
2.4582 to 1.4634 and raises the edge-preservation index from
0.5948 to 0.8218.
Index Terms—Despeckling, Fourier reﬁnement, self-supervised
adaptation, synthetic aperture radar, Transformer .
I. I NTRODUCTION
In synthetic aperture radar (SAR), speckle obscures edges,
texture, and bright scatterers. Despeckling must restore them
despite signal-dependent noise and scarce clean references [1].
Unit-mean Gamma corruption enables controlled multilook
tests, but focused SAR images also contain spatially correlated
speckle and processing artifacts; models trained on simulated
pairs therefore often transfer poorly to real observations.
Lee ﬁltering uses local statistics [2]; SAR-BM3D uses
nonlocal recurrence and transform-domain shrinkage [3]. Su-
pervised CNNs learn paired priors [4], and SAR-CAM adds
continuous multiscale attention [5]. Trans-SAR couples a
hierarchical Transformer encoder with convolutional recon-
struction [6]; later models add dynamic gates and noise-
guided multiscale fusion [7], [8]. SAR-FDD and DA TNet use
frequency decomposition alongside spatial processing [7], [9],
while transformed conditional diffusion addresses multiplica-
tive statistics [10]. Spectral overlap between speckle and scene
structure motivates joint local and full-spectrum reﬁnement
without predeﬁned bands.
For real observations, SAR2SAR uses multitemporal ac-
quisitions [11]; Speckle2V oid combines blind-spot likelihood
training and decorrelation [12]; Speckle2Self uses masked
self-supervision with attention [13]. SDS-SAR forms intensity-
only pairs by correlation-aware sampling [14], whereas
SDUDNet uses unpaired clean and noisy images [15].

Our extension adds six Fourier-domain reﬁnement (FDR)
blocks at the bottleneck and ﬁve decoder scales, combining lo-
cal ﬁltering with full-spectrum real–imaginary channel mixing.
Exact log inversion precedes residual intensity compensation.
Encoder-frozen masked post-adaptation (AMS) then calibrates
reconstruction on noisy real SAR. Fig. 1 separates inherited
operators from additions. Ablations assess the components
across four look numbers; external testing evaluates adaptation
on 20 ICEYE acquisitions.
II. P ROPOSED METHOD
A. Observation and Reconstruction Path
Let X, Y ∈ [0, 1]H×W denote a clean intensity proxy
and its noisy observation. Synthetic pairs use independent
Gamma speckle [6] with look number L. We clip the simulated
intensities to [0, 1]:
Y = clip(X ⊙ N , 0, 1),
Nij ∼ Gamma(L, L−1). (1)
Speckle has unit mean and variance L−1 before clipping. The
normalized logarithmic mapping and its exact inverse are
Tα(Y ) = log(1 + αY ) log(1 + α) , (2)
T −1 α ( ˆZ) = exp[ ˆZ log(1 + α)] − 1 α . (3)
The mapping moderates bright intensities.
Trans-SAR supplies overlap embeddings, spatial-reduction
attention, and basic upsampling [6]. Its ﬁve encoder stages
have widths (32, 64, 128, 320, 512), head counts (1, 1, 2, 4,
8), depths (3, 3, 4, 6, 3), and resolutions from 1282 to 82. After
bottleneck reﬁnement, ﬁve decoder stages apply upsampling,
available skip fusion, FDR, residual convolution, and guidance
modulation in that order. A sigmoid head predicts bounded log
intensity for inversion before intensity compensation.

C. Supervision and Masked Adaptation
Supervised learning combines mean squared error and total
variation (TV) [6]:
Lsup = 1 HW ∥ ˆX − X∥2 2 + λsup TV TV( ˆX). (7)
TV is the sum of the mean absolute horizontal and vertical
neighbor differences. The TV weights balance data ﬁdelity
and spatial smoothness and are selected separately on the cor-
responding validation splits (Section III-B). Following masked
self-supervision [13], our AMS implementation masks pixels
independently with probability 0.2 and uses their original noisy
values as targets for masked L1 loss with TV on the full
prediction:
Ym = Y ⊙ (1 − M ), ˆXm = fθ(Ym),
LAMS = ∥M ⊙ ( ˆXm − Y )∥1 max(∥M ∥1, 1) + λAMS TV( ˆXm). (8)
Mask ones identify masked pixels; outputs are intensities. With
only the Transformer encoder frozen, the bottleneck, decoder,
guidance, head, and compensator adapt for eight epochs at
2 × 10−6. Training masks are resampled and validation masks
ﬁxed. AMS supplies Ym throughout the network, including
compensation; inference uses unmasked observations.
III. E XPERIMENTS
A. Datasets and Evaluation Protocol
NWPU-RESISC45 [17] supplies 900 training and 225 vali-
dation images (20/5 per class), using squared grayscale values
as clean intensity proxies. Approximately 50,000 training

presentations balance L ∈ { 1, 2, 4, 8} deterministically, with
paired ﬂips and right-angle rotations. Four ﬁxed realizations
per validation source yield 900 pairs.
The 2,100 UCMerced images from 21 classes [18] are held
out from model selection and yield 8,400 ﬁxed test pairs, one
corruption per source and look. Images are bilinearly resized to
256 × 256, if needed, before applying (1) once. This evaluates
transfer between optical collections under simulated speckle.
Mendeley dataset fs455tz88y, version 1 [19], supplies 1,175
adaptation-training parents and 146 validation parents, yield-
ing 4,700 and 584 patches. All four patches from a 512 × 512
parent stay in one split. External testing uses 20 complete
ICEYE Open Data acquisitions [20], separate from Mendeley
and excluded from training, validation, and model selection.
These replace the earlier 148-parent internal test evaluation.
NWPU training ﬁts the supervised model, whose selected
checkpoint initializes real-SAR adaptation. V alidation follows
every epoch: minimum intensity mean squared error (MSE)
selects the supervised checkpoint, and minimum masked loss
selects AMS. We ﬁx weights and hyperparameters before
evaluating the independent synthetic and real-SAR test sets.
Methods share scene IDs, native SLC pixel grids, and
decoded linear-intensity inputs. Method-speciﬁc normalization
is inverted before measurement; display stretching is excluded.
Contextual inference tiles are assembled into complete scene
predictions before evaluation. Each scene contributes one score
per metric and receives equal weight.
B. Implementation, Baselines, and Metrics
We use Adam for 50 epochs (approximately 50,000 up-
dates), with batch size 1, initial rate 2 × 10−4, and weight
decay 10−5. V alidation plateaus halve the rate, down to 10−6.
Synthetic validation selects α = 10 from {1, 5, 10, 20, 50} by
PSNR, supported by SSIM. In the separate validation sweeps
(Table II), λsup TV = 0.03 gives the highest macro PSNR/SSIM,
with similar scores at 0.05; λAMS = 10 −3 minimizes M-
index. Larger AMS weights worsen M-index and reduce EPI.
These weights remain ﬁxed for testing (Tables I and V). One
pre-adaptation checkpoint (Full/Ours-base) handles all looks.
NWPU validation selects Lee-MMSE’s window; SAR-BM3D
takes square-root input and squares its output. Both receive
nominal L (oracle-L); SAR-CAM [5], Trans-SAR [6], and
ours receive only corrupted intensity.

E. Ablation Study
Table IV compares representation, compensation, and FDR
variants.
The FDR ablations retain the log transform, inverse, com-
pensator, and local bottleneck branch. Removing bottleneck
and decoder FDR reduces PSNR by 1.01 and 1.47 dB,
respectively. Removing all six FDR blocks reduces PSNR by
1.68 dB and SSIM by 0.0179.
Full exceeds Log-only by 5.92 dB and 0.0670 SSIM,
supporting compensation with FDR present. Log-only yields
lower PSNR but higher SSIM than Intensity-only.

G. Scene-Wise No-Reference Evaluation on Real SAR
From each of the same 20 parents, one 256 × 256 re-
gion is selected for each appearance type: homogeneous (H),
structural (S), and textured (T), giving 60 regions. Input-
only candidate screening favors low spatial variation for H,
coherent boundaries for S, and distributed variation for T.
Noisy-input visual review ﬁxes nonoverlapping coordinates
before any model output is examined. Regions are extracted
from complete scene predictions; each category averages 20
parents, and Average weights the three category means equally.
AMS lowers M-index and increases EPI and RGPI in all
three categories (Table VI). Across category means, M-index
changes from 2.6092 to 1.4327 EPI from 0.4445 to 0.7042,
and RGPI from 0.1733 to 0.3515. ENL falls from 192.04 to
39.24 and remains above Noisy in each category, showing that
the observed change is not simply stronger smoothing.
H. Limitations
The real-data ﬁndings describe no-reference performance
on 20 external ICEYE acquisitions. Clean-target ﬁdelity, non-
linear contrast effects, and scene-to-scene uncertainty are not
established by these mean scores.
